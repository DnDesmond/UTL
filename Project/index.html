Investigation and Research:

Title:

To estimate changes in and predict risk to insect populations considering relevant environmental variables.

Thesis statement:

Upon hearing the problem presented and the themes associated, my first thought was the modelling of animal populations and how they react to the adjustment of various environmental effects. In order for me to work with this I shall have to consider various populations, how realistic it is to simulate the habitats of these populations and whether it's feasible for me to model multiple interdependent populations or if I should stick to a single population and focus more heavily on the environmental factors inclement upon their saturation. Obviously it would be preferable for me to model multiple but the limitations of the micropython language and the time limit may restrict to a single more detailed simulation.

(Insects):Chorthippus Brunneus

Relevant effects of environmental variables on animal populations:

Possible Variables:

    - Humidity

    - Temperature

    - Tree Cover

    - Soil Moisture Level

    - Rainfall

The effects of a relatively high temperature can include increased growth and development rate, a potential additional instar growth stage, increased mass and size, an increased number of egg pods per female and an advanced rate at which eggs reach maturity.

The effects of an excessively low temperature are far more impactful, with an extreme loss in survivability accompanying falling temperatures; and a complete loss of egg gestation at temperatures below 20 degrees.

High soil moisture seems to have neither negative nor positive impacts but particularly low soil moisture is linked to increased hatching time, a loss in survival rates for incubation and other negative effects on the development of larvae.

An unfortunate side effect found in the study of environmental sciences is that there are rarely existing solutions, while I can make reference to studies conducted, the relations between technology and how we can use it to study the environment are still new and being explored. It is my hope that this project can stand as an example for future feasibility studies and researches into the unseen naiveté of potential projects.

My model works to intake the data from the microbit and to return a reasonable value for the risk of extinction or mass death of the C. Brunneus genus in the range 0 to 1 with 1 meriting full extinction. It values the effect of soil moisture on the risk of extinction as being circa half of the deciding factor and directly proportional, due to its incredible effect on the survival rates as shown in the investigation portion. It also looks at temperature in brackets and changes the risk value depending on where it falls, giving spikes in risk for temperatures below 20 and above thirty, as these temperatures are dangerous to C. Brunneus eggs and larvae. It then takes the temperature, soil moisture and calculated risk values and plots them on a live updating graph, adjusted for the visibility of the data. Risk is multiplied to make it visible alongside the obviously larger values for temperature and soil moisture. The data taken in is obviously heavily multiplied to make it fit visual aesthetics for readability.
